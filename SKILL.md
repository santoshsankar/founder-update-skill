# Founder Monthly Update

Helps a founder draft their monthly investor update by gathering real context from wherever it already lives, rather than starting from a blank page.

## Why this exists

Most of the busywork in a monthly update is retrieval, not writing: what happened with customers, what shipped, who joined or left, what the numbers are. That information already lives in email, Slack, and the CRM. This skill pulls it together so the founder spends time on the parts that actually need judgment: Customers, Product, People, Major Goals, and Shoutouts. See `references/template.md` for the full field-by-field guide and the reasoning behind the section order.

## Step 1: Check what's actually connected

Before anything else, check which of these are available as connected tools in this session:

- **Email** (Gmail or similar)
- **Slack**
- **CRM** (Attio, HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, or whatever the founder uses — don't assume any one of these specifically)

Don't assume all three are connected. Check the tool list first. For anything not connected, don't stall the draft waiting on it. Ask the founder for a quick manual input instead, or offer to check the connector directory if one of these tools isn't set up yet and the founder wants it connected.

## Step 2: Pull context from what's connected

For each connected source, gather only what's useful for this update, going back to the date of the last update. Ask the founder when that was if it isn't clear; default to 30 days back.

**Email**
- Customer threads that indicate a win (expansion, renewal, strong feedback) or a risk (complaint, silence after a good start, a churn signal)
- Any commitments made to investors in the last update that need a status
- Recent recruiting threads (an offer out, a candidate who accepted or declined)

**Slack**
- Product ship announcements or launch threads
- Customer support escalations or recurring complaints
- Hiring, onboarding, or departure threads
- Anything that reads like a genuine win worth naming, or a real blocker worth naming

**CRM**
- New customers closed since the last update, with deal size if available
- Deals that moved backward: stalled, downgraded, or lost
- Pipeline volume and quality relative to the last update, if the founder tracks that

If a source isn't connected, ask the founder directly rather than guessing. "What's the one or two biggest customer stories this month, good or bad?" works fine as a substitute for CRM access.

## Step 3: Get what nothing above can give you

Revenue, burn, cash in bank, and runway rarely live in email, Slack, or a CRM. Ask the founder for these directly, or check whether a finance or cap table tool is connected instead. The Asks and Major Goals sections also need to come from the founder directly. Those require their own judgment about what they need and what matters next, not something inferred from a tool.

## Step 4: Draft the update

Follow the section order and guidance in `references/template.md`: Asks, KPIs, Customers, Product, People, Capital, Major Goals, Shoutouts. Keep the whole thing readable in under two minutes. Write in the founder's own voice as much as it can be inferred from their emails or Slack messages, not a generic corporate tone. Be honest in the Customers and Product sections specifically. A win with no corresponding risk mentioned anywhere reads as an incomplete month, not a good one.

## Step 5: Hand it back for edits

Present the draft and ask what to adjust. Founders usually want to sharpen the Asks and rewrite Major Goals themselves. Don't be precious about the first draft. It exists to remove the blank-page problem, not to be final.
